Why Capability

Adopting new tools is not the same as building new capability.

Companies are experimenting with AI, but progress can remain limited to a few enthusiasts. Without support, repetition and a shared view of progress, experiments may never become reliable working practices.

Capability takes learning out of the classroom and into the work itself.

The pilot journey

From the starting point to changed practice.

An approximately 90-day organisational pilot connects each participant's own work, a digital coaching service and a structured evaluation. It starts with a focused task and builds on what people learn while doing real work.

01

Choose the right work

Agree on the organisational goal and identify participant tasks where AI could make a meaningful difference. Establish the starting point and what observable progress could look like.

02

Build practice through doing

Participants experiment, evaluate and repeat new approaches in their own tasks. The digital coaching service helps them take the next step, while a Prompted human contact supports the organisation.

03

Make change visible

Review what was observed in work, whether new practices were repeated and whether learning transferred to new situations. Provide a privacy-bounded organisational summary to inform the next decision.

Two experiences

Support for people doing the work. Evidence for people leading it.

For participants

Coaching alongside real work.

The learner starts with a task that matters to them. A digital coaching service helps frame the challenge, try an approach, check the result and improve it. Participants can see their own progress and develop at a sustainable pace.

For the organisation

A grounded view of what can be evidenced.

The sponsor receives an agreed, restricted view of progress and published group-level evidence. Private coaching conversations and raw work text are not shared as buyer reports. Evidence is reviewed through first signals, repetition, transfer and independence.

What the organisation gains

Beyond attendance. A stronger basis for the next decision.

01

A focused starting point

Connect the pilot to specific work tasks and an organisational goal, rather than generic tool training.

02

Support in the flow of work

Digital coaching supports day-to-day learning, with a named human contact from Prompted for the organisation.

03

A credible summary

An agreed, bounded report of observed evidence and areas requiring further development may be released to the sponsor.

A focused pilot

Small enough to start. Clear enough to evaluate.

The initial model is around 20 participants and an approximately 90-day organisational evaluation period. This is not a mandatory completion deadline for an individual learner.

Goals, participant count, human support, deliverables, schedule and pricing are agreed in the proposal. The pilot does not guarantee financial returns, individual certification or immediate change across the entire organisation.
